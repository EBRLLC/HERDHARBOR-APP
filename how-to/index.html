HerdHarbor How To Center

Get the job done without hunting through the app.

Step-by-step help for the current HerdHarbor workflow—from adding an animal and building a pedigree to recording births, health care, genetics, sales, subscriptions, referrals, and cloud sync.

Current for Alpha v1.8.1. HerdHarbor uses an animal-first workflow: open an animal when the job is specific to that animal, and use Dashboard/Quick Add for day-to-day work.

01

Getting Started

Set up the operation once, then work from Dashboard, Animals, or Quick Add.

Finish your operation profile. Enter the farm, rabbitry, homestead, or operation name and the adult account holder information requested during registration.
Add your first animals. Use Animals or Quick Add. Enter factual information you know; do not guess missing pedigree, health, or genetic information.
Use Dashboard for today’s work. Dashboard combines existing tasks and dated follow-ups so you can see what needs attention without maintaining another list.
Use the animal profile for animal-specific work. Open the animal first, then choose the relevant Health, Breeding, Genetics, Pedigree, Shows, Production, or History action when available.
Tip: HerdHarbor follows an “enter once, reuse everywhere” rule. When a fact already exists in a record, downstream tools should reuse it rather than asking you to create a duplicate.
02

Animals & Animal Profiles

The animal profile is the normal starting point for work tied to one animal.

Open Animals and choose Add Animal. Enter name/tag, species, sex, breed, birth information, and other known identifying details.
Save the animal before adding related records. This gives Health, Breeding, Pedigree, Genetics, Sales, and Analytics a canonical animal to reference.
Open the animal profile. Relevant tabs and actions appear based on species and available data. Not every animal needs every tab.
Change status instead of deleting history. Use Sold, Deceased, Archived, or Ancestor Only when appropriate so historical records and pedigrees remain intact.
03

How to Create a Pedigree

Build the ancestry around an existing animal and review it before saving or printing.

Open the animal, then choose Pedigree. You can also open the Pedigrees area and select the subject animal.
Choose how you want to build it. Enter ancestors manually or use the pedigree document/import workflow when you have a source document.
Match existing ancestors whenever possible. Reuse an animal already in HerdHarbor instead of creating duplicate ancestry records.
Fill only verified ancestor details. Names, registration numbers, colors, breeds, and other fields should reflect the source you actually have.
Review the pedigree preview. Check sire/dam placement and each generation before saving.
Save, then print or export from the pedigree view. The saved pedigree remains tied to the subject animal and can be reused by genetics and breeding tools where supported.
Best practice: If an ancestor already exists as an active, archived, sold, or ancestor-only animal, match that record rather than typing the same animal again.
04

How to Record a Breeding

Create the pairing once so later birth and offspring records inherit the correct parents.

Open Breeding or choose Breed from an animal profile. Select the sire and dam from your existing animal records.
Enter the breeding date and known details. Add notes or relevant dates without inventing pregnancy results or outcomes that have not occurred.
Save the breeding record. HerdHarbor uses that record for breeding history, due-date workflows, reminders, and the later birth/litter flow.
Update the existing breeding as events happen. Use the same workflow for checks, due status, or birth rather than making unrelated duplicate records.
05

How to Enter a New Birth or Litter

Start from the breeding when one exists so sire and dam remain connected automatically.

Open Births & Litters or the breeding record. Choose the breeding that produced the birth whenever possible.
Record the birth date and outcome. Enter the counts and other species-appropriate birth information shown by HerdHarbor.
Create offspring from the litter workflow. Add individual names/tags when known. Parentage should carry forward from the selected breeding.
Save the litter and offspring. New individual animals become available for Health, Growth, Pedigree, Genetics, Shows, Production, and Sales as applicable.
Use Tasks for follow-up work. Weaning or other scheduled care can appear in Today when it is represented by existing task/follow-up data.
06

How to Enter Health & Medical Data

Use routine Health records for events and Health Intelligence for ongoing episodes when available.

Open the animal and choose Health. You can also use Health or Quick Add when recording care across the operation.
Choose the correct record type. Record the actual event—such as weight/measurement, routine care, medication, vaccination, treatment, observation, or other supported health entry.
Enter the date and factual details. Include product/medication, amount, route, notes, provider, or other fields only when known and relevant.
Add follow-up information when needed. Recheck dates, booster dates, quarantine, withdrawal end dates, and Health Intelligence follow-ups can feed the Today queue.
Use a Health Episode for an ongoing problem. When Health Intelligence is available, start an episode and add care over time instead of scattering an ongoing issue across unrelated notes.
Health note: HerdHarbor is a recordkeeping and educational tool, not a substitute for veterinary diagnosis or treatment. Use the Symptom Guide for educational triage information and contact a veterinarian for medical decisions.
07

How to Record Weight & Use Growth Charts

Weights are health measurements first; analytics reads those records rather than maintaining a second set.

Open the animal and choose Add Weight/Health measurement. Enter the date and measured weight using the displayed unit.
Save each real measurement once. Do not duplicate a weight in Analytics; charts should derive from the health measurement history.
Open Analytics and use the Growth/Weight view. Select the animal/species and date range you want to review.
Use trends, not one data point. Growth charts are most useful when measurements are taken consistently enough for your management purpose.
08

How to Use Genetics

Open Genetics from the animal so HerdHarbor can route to the correct species engine.

Open the animal profile and choose Genetics. HerdHarbor routes rabbits to the rabbit genetics tools and supported other species to the multi-species engine.
Enter only known phenotype/genotype information. Unknown genes should remain unknown; a prediction is not the same as a confirmed genotype.
Use pairing/prediction tools as planning aids. Review probabilities and compatibility information before making breeding decisions.
Keep pedigree and genetics distinct. Pedigree records ancestry; Genetics evaluates inherited traits using the information available.
09

Dashboard, Today & Tasks

Use the operational queue to answer “What needs attention?”

Start on Dashboard. Review Today before moving into individual modules.
Work from the source record. A Health recheck, show deadline, breeding/weaning task, quarantine date, or other due item comes from its canonical HerdHarbor record.
Create manual Tasks for work that is not already represented elsewhere. Avoid creating a duplicate task when an existing dated record already supplies the reminder.
Complete or update the underlying work. This keeps Dashboard useful rather than turning it into a disconnected checklist.
10

Analytics, Shows & Production

Analytics summarizes records already stored in HerdHarbor.

Enter operational records in their normal module. Weights belong in Health, sales in Sales, show results in Shows, and production in Production.
Open Analytics to review trends. Use available views for overview, breeding, sales/revenue, growth/weight, shows, production, and health measurements when relevant data exists.
Filter to the question you are answering. Choose animal, species, date range, or other available filters instead of mixing unrelated records.
Treat Market Analytics as optional. Participation remains consent-based; operational records continue to work without opting into market contribution.
11

Sales & Customers

Keep the buyer, transaction, payment, and animal status connected.

Create or select the customer. Use the existing buyer record when the customer has purchased from you before.
Create the sale and select the animal/items. Enter the real sale date, price, and payment information.
Record payments against the sale. This preserves the transaction history rather than replacing the original sale total.
Mark transferred animals Sold/Transferred through the normal workflow. Historical pedigree, health, breeding, and production data remains part of your records.

Subscription & Referral Program v1.8.1

Current launch policy, billing controls, and qualified referral credits.

Launch trial runs through September 30, 2026. Ordinary accounts receive Member-level access during the launch window that began September 6, 2026.
Paid subscriptions begin October 1, 2026. Member is $14.99/month. Junior remains free; Business is Coming Soon.
Manage billing from the Subscription/Account area. Checkout and Customer Portal actions use Stripe; active paid entitlement is synchronized back to HerdHarbor.
Share your public Referral ID. The Referral ID is separate from your authentication/database identifier.
A referral qualifies after the referred Member’s first successful monthly renewal. The initial subscription payment does not qualify the referral.
Every five qualified referrals earns one Member-month credit. Credits stack and can cover a future monthly renewal without changing the normal billing-cycle anchor.
If a paid plan ends: your records are preserved. Accounts without another qualifying entitlement fall back to Junior rather than having their data deleted.
13

Cloud Sync & Offline Use

HerdHarbor maintains protected account storage while preserving offline-capable app behavior.

Watch the sync indicator. The sidebar/top bar reports whether HerdHarbor is checking, syncing, or working from protected account storage.
You can continue supported work offline. The app keeps an offline copy on the device and synchronizes changes when connectivity returns.
Allow sync to finish before switching devices when possible. This reduces the chance of creating competing edits to the same record.
Do not treat browser storage as your only backup. Sign in to the correct HerdHarbor account and confirm cloud sync for records you need across devices.
14

Youth & Junior Use

HerdHarbor accounts are adult-managed; youth participation requires parent/legal-guardian supervision or approval.

An adult account holder completes registration. New registration requires the adult account holder to self-report age 18+ and complete the required attestations.
Youth use stays supervised. Parents/legal guardians should review records, account access, purchases, communications, and submitted information.
Junior is the free plan. It retains the existing five-active-animal transition limit while previously stored records remain preserved.
Use youth/show educational guides as learning aids. Program rules from 4-H, FFA, breed associations, fairs, or shows remain authoritative for those programs.

Common Questions

Fast answers to the issues that usually cause confusion.

Why don’t I see every tab on every animal?

Animal-profile tabs are contextual. HerdHarbor only shows actions relevant to the animal/species and the data or engines available for that record.

Should I enter the same weight in Health and Analytics?

No. Enter the measurement once in the canonical Health/measurement workflow. Analytics should read that record.

Should I create a new litter without selecting the breeding?

If a breeding record exists, start from or select it so sire and dam information carries forward correctly. Use a standalone birth/litter only when there truly is no applicable breeding record.

Will I lose records if I fall back to Junior?

No. The v1.8.1 subscription policy preserves previously stored records. Junior limits active-animal transitions rather than deleting your history.

Is the Symptom Guide veterinary advice?

No. It is educational information for recognizing urgency and organizing observations. Veterinary diagnosis and treatment decisions belong with a veterinarian.

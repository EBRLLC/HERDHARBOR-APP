What Makes a Good Breeding Record?

Published October 7, 2026 · Beginner · About 3 min read

Short version: A good breeding record lets you reconstruct the entire reproductive event without relying on memory: who was paired, when, why, what happened, what offspring resulted, what complications occurred, and whether the cross should be repeated.

Record the pair and the date

Use unique animal IDs for both parents and record every exposure or breeding date. If animals are pasture- or pen-exposed over a range of dates, record the exposure window rather than inventing a single breeding date.

Record the reason for the mating

A short breeding objective is one of the most valuable fields in the record. It gives you something specific to evaluate later and prevents hindsight from turning any outcome into a success.

Track reproductive milestones

  • Breeding or exposure dates.
  • Pregnancy check and result when used.
  • Expected due, calving, lambing, kidding, farrowing, or kindling window.
  • Actual birth date.
  • Number born and number alive, where relevant.
  • Sexes, IDs, and parent links for offspring.
  • Weaning date or other species-relevant milestone.

Failures belong in the record too

A breeding that does not result in pregnancy is still useful data. Record failed exposures, repeats, suspected pregnancy loss, infertile eggs where applicable, and other unsuccessful outcomes. Removing failures from the record makes fertility look better than it really is.

Document complications and interventions

Record assisted births, veterinary care, fostering, rejected offspring, poor milk or nursing, abnormal discharge, retained placenta, reproductive injury, or other complications appropriate to the species. Note who made the observation and what action followed.

Link offspring instead of copying them into notes

Each offspring should have its own record linked to the breeding event and parents. That allows later performance, health, and breeding outcomes to flow back to the original mating without duplicating data.

Close the breeding record with an evaluation

After enough time has passed to judge the result, summarize whether the mating met the objective. Did the offspring improve the target trait? Did a fault repeat? Was the litter or cohort consistent? Would you repeat the cross, modify it, or avoid it?

This turns the breeding log into a decision tool instead of a calendar.

Bottom line

The strongest breeding record captures the plan, the reproductive outcome, the offspring, and the lesson. If you can use last year’s record to make a better mating this year, the record is doing its job.
